Research

One question runs through my work: why do some regions develop new capabilities, firms and activities while others stay locked into fragile paths, and which policies can change that? I combine Brazilian administrative microdata (RAIS, SECEX, CAGED), modern causal identification and economic complexity, used as a mechanism rather than as an index.

Microfoundations of economic complexity

Where do relatedness and economic complexity come from? I derive the link between a region and an activity from a matching function with frictions: the O-Ring form appears as the joint probability that tasks are matched, and Hidalgo's capability model is the frictionless limit.

Industrial policy, suppliers and regional development

Do large investments and industrial instruments, such as subsidised credit, tax incentives and public procurement, build lasting local capabilities and suppliers, or only a temporary expansion?

Firms, market power and local labour markets

Does regional concentration raise productivity and scale, or does it strengthen employer power, reduce mobility and widen territorial inequality?
